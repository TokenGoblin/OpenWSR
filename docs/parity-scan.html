OpenWSR Parity Scan

OpenWSR already decodes and renders radar as well as anything on the market — the gaps are almost entirely layers it doesn't draw and controls it doesn't expose, not physics it can't do. Every critical missing data source turns out to be free, public, and unauthenticated; the real blockers are one file format and one afternoon of UI work.

Where OpenWSR already leads

Worth stating first, because it shapes what's worth chasing: several things we give away are sold as premium tiers elsewhere, and two have no commercial equivalent at all.

Unlimited Level II archive

Any site, any day back to 1991, free. RadarScope gates its historical archive behind Pro Tier Two.

Sub-scan live latency

Partial volumes render as each tilt completes — roughly five seconds after the radar sweeps it, not after the full scan.

Zero-cost product switching

Geometry is generated in the vertex shader, so changing moment or palette is a texture swap. Measured 0.7–11 ms.

National hotspot finder

One click scans all 163 sites and flies to the heaviest cell in the country. No competitor ships this.

Track-based proximity alerts

Alerts carry a real ETA from the forecast track, not just "a warning was issued near you".

No account, no key, no tier

Runs anonymously against public data. Supercell Wx has five open issues about its mandatory paid map keys.

UI findings

From inspecting the running app's live control tree and layout rather than reading the XAML. The first four are defects, not preferences — one is a bug I introduced.

01

There is no colour scale anywhere on screen

The app renders dBZ, m/s, dB and correlation coefficient in six different palettes and never once tells you what the colours mean. A user cannot convert what they see into a number without already knowing the table by heart. Every competitor shows a scale; it is the single most conspicuous omission in the interface.

FixVertical colour bar docked to the map's left edge, labelled at palette breakpoints, switching automatically with the product and with any imported .pal. The palette data structure already carries everything needed to draw it.
02

Data age reads "116106 h 20 m" on archive data

The age indicator measures wall-clock distance from the displayed sweep, which is correct for live data and absurd for a 2013 case study. Observed live in the running app. It also renders in grey rather than green or amber in archive mode, so the one genuinely safety-relevant readout in the product looks broken exactly when someone is learning to trust it.

FixShow elapsed time only in live mode. In archive mode show the absolute scan time and label the mode explicitly.
03

Toolbar overflow is disabled, so controls can become unreachable

Nine control groups sit in a single non-wrapping row, and the overflow chevron is explicitly collapsed in ToolBar_Loaded — my doing, on the assumption everything fits. At narrow window widths the right-hand controls are clipped with no way to reach them.

FixRe-enable the overflow chevron immediately, then reorganise: the toolbar currently mixes source selection, playback, layer toggles, and app actions with no grouping a user could predict.
04

Keyboard shortcuts are advertised inside the status text

Every status message ends with [R/V/W/D/P/C moment, ↑/↓ tilt], permanently consuming status-bar width to document six shortcuts, while the tilt and product actually in use are buried in the same run-on string.

FixProduct and tilt become real controls — a segmented product selector and a vertical tilt picker on the map edge, showing the VCP's actual elevation angles. Shortcuts move to a help overlay on ?.
05

Time is controlled from two places at once

Loop and speed live in the top toolbar; the scrub slider lives in a separate strip above the status bar. One concept, two locations, neither aware of the other's state. The slider itself has no ticks, no time labels, and no indication of which frames are cached versus still downloading.

FixOne full-width timeline: inline play/pause and speed, hour ticks with labels, cached-frame shading, and a distinct marker for "now" — the standard pattern every consumer radar app converged on, and the piece that would make future radar legible later.
06

The status bar is eight fields of run-on text

Site, scan time, product, elevation, cut index, shortcut list, frame rate, upload latency, data age, and the full inspector readout share one line with no visual hierarchy. The developer diagnostics and the meteorology sit at identical weight.

FixSplit them: meteorological readout follows the cursor on the map, feed health gets a compact fixed slot, and render diagnostics move behind a debug toggle.
07

The layers panel is a flat column of checkboxes

Twelve controls with two sliders and bold text as the only grouping. No per-layer opacity, no colour swatches tying a checkbox to what it draws on the map, no way to reorder, and no indication of which layers are currently returning data versus quietly empty.

FixLayer rows with a swatch, a name, a visibility toggle, and a per-layer opacity control — with a live/stale dot per layer once satellite, lightning and mosaic arrive and layer count doubles.
08

Failure and empty states are status-bar strings

"No volumes for this site on this date", a failed S3 list, and a Level III fetch error all surface as one line of grey text that scrolls away on the next update. The map keeps showing whatever was there before, so a stale display and a healthy one look identical.

FixPersistent, dismissible state on the map itself for anything that means "what you are looking at is not current".
09

Nothing explains the storm symbols

Diamonds, dotted forecast tracks, cones, triangles sized by hail probability, and yellow mesocyclone rings all appear with no key. The information is genuinely dense and genuinely good — and unreadable to anyone who didn't build it.

FixA compact symbol key in the layers panel, next to the toggles that draw each symbol.
10

First launch has nothing to show

The app opens on a hard-coded 2013 case study at KTLX. That is a fine demo and a strange default: it isn't the user's weather, isn't current, and makes the data-age readout look broken (see finding 02).

FixOpen on the user's location if home is set, otherwise offer the hotspot scan we already have — "show me the weather that matters right now" is a better first impression than any static default.
11

The window doesn't adapt

The layers panel is a fixed 212 px that neither collapses nor scrolls independently, and the default 1280×800 window leaves the map at roughly 1050 px wide once the panel and chrome take their share.

FixCollapsible panel with a persisted state, and a keyboard shortcut to hide all chrome for a clean full-bleed map.

Every missing source is free — I checked today

This is the encouraging part. I hit each endpoint anonymously while writing this; all seven responded. Nothing below needs an account, a key, or a card.

SourceEndpointFormatCadenceUnlocks
MRMS mosaic noaa-mrms-pds GRIB2.gz ~2 min Seamless national reflectivity, precip rate, rotation tracks
Iowa State mosaic tiles mesonet.agron.iastate.edu PNG XYZ ~5 min A national view with no GRIB2 work — drops into our existing tile pipeline
GOES ABI noaa-goes19 NetCDF 5–10 min Visible, infrared, water vapour satellite
GOES GLM noaa-goes19/GLM-L2-LCFA NetCDF 20 s Lightning — RadarScope's flagship paid feature
HRRR noaa-hrrr-bdp-pds GRIB2 hourly Future radar / nowcast out to 18 hours
SPC outlooks spc.noaa.gov/products/outlook GeoJSON 6×/day Categorical, tornado, hail, wind outlooks
api.weather.gov already integrated GeoJSON 60 s Watches, statements, discussions — we already call it and discard these

One dependency gates four features. MRMS, HRRR, satellite and lightning all arrive as GRIB2 or NetCDF, and we currently decode neither. Supercell Wx is stuck at exactly this point — its GRIB2 issue blocks its mosaic, wind and precipitation-type work, and it has been open for two years. The pre-rendered Iowa State tiles are the way around it: a national mosaic in an afternoon, using the tile cache we already built, while the format work happens on its own schedule rather than blocking everything behind it.

Suggested sequence

Ordered by user-visible value per unit of work. The first group is genuinely a day or two and changes how the app feels more than anything after it.

01

Make what we already have legible

~2 days
  • Colour scale docked to the map, product-aware, honouring imported palettes.
  • Settings screen — tile provider, map key, NWS contact string, units. Removes the JSON editing and fixes our out-of-spec User-Agent.
  • Location search by city or ZIP, resolving to the nearest radar. We already have the site table and the nearest-site maths.
  • Data-age fix, toolbar overflow re-enabled, storm symbol key.
  • Unified timeline with ticks, labels and inline transport controls.
02

Layers that need no new file formats

~1 week
  • National mosaic via Iowa State tiles — the single biggest perceived gap, closed cheaply.
  • Watches, statements, mesoscale discussions from the alerts endpoint we already poll.
  • SPC outlooks and local storm reports, both plain GeoJSON.
  • County and state boundaries as a real layer instead of whatever the basemap happens to draw.
  • Windows notifications so proximity alerts work when the app isn't focused.
03

The format work

~2–3 weeks
  • GRIB2 decoder — unlocks native MRMS and HRRR together.
  • NetCDF reader — unlocks GOES satellite and GLM lightning together.
  • Future radar on the timeline built in phase 01, running past "now".
04

Depth for the people who care most

open-ended
  • Vertical cross-section — the standout gap across the entire open-source field, and we already hold the volumes it needs.
  • Storm-relative velocity with a user-settable motion vector, then velocity dealiasing.
  • Placefile support, inheriting the GR community's overlay ecosystem wholesale.
  • GIF export, drawing tools, azimuthal shear.
